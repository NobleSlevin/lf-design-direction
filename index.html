Tone & feeling

Established, serious, trusted. The kind of brand that doesn't need to shout. Photography anchors emotional weight; type provides intellectual authority. Gold is used sparingly — a single CTA button, an icon tint — never decorative. Spacing is the design.

Watch-outs

Can read as cold or austere if the photography doesn't carry genuine warmth. The gold accent loses impact if overused — treat it as a single signature note per viewport. Ensure sufficient contrast between the gold and the dark backgrounds it appears on.

Modern Authority

Deep forest green or midnight navy as the hero canvas. Warm cream/parchment as secondary surface. Rounded pill CTAs introduce softness that prevents the palette from feeling cold. Mixes editorial serif with clean sans — approachable authority.

Tone & feeling

Professional warmth. The forest green grounds authority while cream surfaces lighten the experience. Pill buttons and floating social-proof chips add modern digital trust signals without sacrificing gravitas. Feels contemporary and human.

Watch-outs

The green/cream palette is increasingly popular — differentiation comes from the specific brand photography and illustration style chosen. Avoid amber overuse; it reads as a highlight colour only. The navy alternate is a useful mid-page section contrast.

Noir Persona

Near-black canvas that's nearly a void. A single portrait photograph takes the entire stage — person, not iconography. Ultra-bold grotesque type bleeds over the image. One accent color dot. This is personal brand energy: one name, one face, total presence.

Tone & feeling

Commanding, singular, unapologetically bold. The design stakes a claim. No stock art — one real face occupies the hero. The philosophy section with large italic serif creates a personal-manifesto moment. High-contrast, low-decoration. Best for a solo practitioner or named personal brand.

Watch-outs

Requires a strong, high-quality portrait — the entire direction collapses without it. The void-like whitespace reads as unfinished if content density is low. The single accent color must be chosen carefully; teal here reads as tech-forward, which may not fit every sector.

Clean SaaS

White and near-white surfaces with an ultra-tight typography system. No hero photography — gradients or abstract shapes fill the hero. Black rounded CTAs. Large pull-quotes carry visual weight. Feels like a B2B tech product positioned in a professional services context.

Tone & feeling

Rational, data-forward, product-confident. The brand positions itself alongside modern B2B SaaS. Typography does all the heavy lifting — no decorative photography. Large pull-quote sections break up scroll with opinion and boldness.

Watch-outs

Can read as generic if not paired with strong product UI screenshots or distinctive illustration. The yellow accent is a differentiator — without it the palette risks feeling plain. Pull quotes need genuinely compelling copy; weak quotes in this treatment read as filler.
